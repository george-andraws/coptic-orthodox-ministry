# Orthodox Iconography

Read the image before interpreting it. Icons are theology in color, but each image also has specific inscriptions, composition, gestures, materials, provenance, and local traditions. Do not guess.

## Identification workflow

1. Inspect the actual image; crop or zoom details when possible.
2. Read inscriptions before assigning identities.
3. Identify the scene, then figures, using labels, halos, vesture, posture, attributes, and placement.
4. Separate confidence levels: established identification, likely reading, and unresolved detail.
5. Explain the image from center outward, then explain its theological meaning.
6. Distinguish historical evidence, later pious tradition, and iconographic convention.

A halo ordinarily marks a holy person, angel, or divine figure; do not call a haloed person demonic. A lower or dark figure may be Hades, death, a defeated enemy, or a heretic, but identify it only from real evidence. In council icons, do not assert a famous confrontation unless the visual evidence or source supports it.

## Using visuals in lessons

Use an icon, map, manuscript, or artwork only when it illuminates the text or doctrine. Name its type clearly: Orthodox icon, Western biblical art, historical map, museum object, or generated teaching aid. An icon may anchor theological reading; Western art may illuminate narrative or emotion but must not be represented as Orthodox iconography.

Record a source page, creator or owner when known, tradition/style, license or rights note, and a short explanation of the visual's teaching value. Prefer Coptic and other Eastern Christian sources where they are relevant; use public-domain or clearly licensed files for reusable publication. Keep slide or teaching directions in a lesson's final teaching guide, not in an image caption.

## Response standard

Lead with the direct identification. Name the visual evidence and uncertainty. Explain theology only after the viewer understands what is shown. Do not claim private research tools, local asset paths, or reuse rights that have not been verified.
