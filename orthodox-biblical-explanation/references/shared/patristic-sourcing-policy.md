# Patristic Sourcing Policy

- Prefer direct patristic texts when practical.
- Keep summary and paraphrase as the interpretive frame, but when exact wording is verified and source status allows it, include verified direct wording that gives the Father's own voice.
- Treat Catena-style tools as discovery aids, not final citation authority, when direct sources are available.
- Paraphrase when exact wording is not verified, source status does not allow reader-facing quotation, the quote would be too long, or citation/provenance is weak.
- Cite works specifically: treatise, homily, book, chapter, section, or paragraph when available.
- Locate the quotation first, then trace backward to its nearest containing work and section heading; corroborate that boundary against the page's contents when available. A collection page can place an unrelated letter immediately before the actual treatise, so a genuine word-for-word match does not authenticate the supplied title. Derive homily numbers from the opened work heading, not a mirror's URL ordinal or document index. A homily can begin with one biblical reference and continue to another; verify its identity and the quoted passage separately. Check that a cited URL fragment actually exists and encloses the quotation within the claimed section: locate its target, the quotation, and the next peer section boundary in the primary HTML. An existing anchor in the wrong chapter is still a bad citation, and a server can return the correct page while ignoring a nonexistent anchor. Keep one verified work/edition/section record per excerpt, then reconcile every occurrence in the prose, Sources list and footnote definitions before freezing; correcting one occurrence does not repair the others. If no verified fragment is available, cite the work URL with the actual biblical lemma, section or printed pages instead of inventing a deep link. Preserve provisional collector filenames and prior review claims as history, but correct the reader citation and evidence locator when the primary heading disagrees.
- Preserve translator-supplied square brackets and the whole source sentence when promoting a review fragment to a quotation; remove online footnote-call numerals and printed page markers as editorial apparatus, not as authorial wording. Check multi-sentence excerpts for contiguity in the cited witness: mark omitted intervening text with an ellipsis or present separate excerpts, because two individually authentic sentences can still form a misleading splice. Follow the actual cited translation's pronouns, capitalization and punctuation rather than combining a supplied quotation bank with a differently modernized mirror. A fragment beginning mid-sentence must not be silently presented as a complete sentence. When a task imposes a short quotation cap, recount the actual candidate excerpt programmatically after editing and interpret `under N words` strictly; a reviewer's count or recommended fragment may violate its own limit. Preserve the source's controlling qualification in an adjacent verified fragment or faithful paraphrase; shortening must not make a Father appear to endorse a broader claim than his argument allows.
- Verify translator, reviser, and editor roles against the exact edition's title page and work-specific preface. Mirror footers can import credits from another work, and collection metadata can misidentify an English reviser as the translator. Match the notice to the assigned passage range: different works in one volume, and different book or chapter ranges within one long work, may have different translators. Record the applicable range and preserve translator, reviser, abridger and general-editor roles separately. Check which volume the preface actually covers before applying its credit to later homilies; a first-volume preface can explicitly leave the remaining translation to another volume. If the quotation and locator are independently sound but the translator remains unresolved, omit or qualify that credit instead of inventing it or discarding the verified quotation.
- Treat discovery metadata as provisional when a multi-work document path, heading, author, or translation credit conflicts with the primary text. Verify the containing work and actual passage before attribution; do not silently repair the corpus as part of a guide-only revision.
- Never invent a Father, quote, title, homily number, citation location, feast usage, or lectionary placement. Verify the actual source supports paraphrases and alleged agreement as well as verbatim quotations. Uncertainty about a named Father cannot become `the Fathers teach`: support the scope of collective attribution or identify a bounded editorial synthesis, qualify, or omit.
- Distinguish received Coptic doctrinal witness, wider Christian exegetical witness, modern pastoral interpretation and disputed/limited-use historical witness. A source's usefulness on a literary question does not establish normative Coptic doctrinal authority. Preserve breadth without implying equal ecclesial reception; sensitive doctrine requires a qualified Coptic theological reader.
- Verify a remembered work or letter in its full available primary text, including linked appendices. A catalogue's immediate subject does not exclude a biblical retelling inside the work. Check extraction completeness before declaring a quotation absent: a successful response with the right title can silently contain only opening paragraphs, a navigation shell, or a truncated body. Require the target passage and enough surrounding argument to identify speaker, qualification and locator; inspect expected final headings or page markers when claiming whole-work coverage. Recover incomplete extraction from the same primary URL before broadening discovery. A tested direct-fetch pattern is `urllib.request.urlopen(urllib.request.Request(primary_url, headers={"User-Agent": "Mozilla/5.0"}), timeout=30).read()`; preserve raw HTML and a full text companion in task evidence, then share located excerpts rather than repeatedly fetching or printing the entire work. Use the browser when direct retrieval still cannot recover the source. Keep a provisional failed lookup distinct from a claim of nonexistence.
- Distinguish an ancient source's authenticity from its suitability for the proposed interpretation. Verify the exact recension containing the match before attributing it to the named Father; an expansion in a longer recension is not authenticated merely because it appears on a genuine source page. Identify who speaks the cited words and inspect the author's response: a work can quote an opponent, disputed creed, historical document or scriptural lemma without endorsing it as the Father's own teaching. Keep adjacent examples distinct, checking which person each praise or descriptor actually concerns before combining excerpts. Follow inline note-calls and their editorial notes at the exact fragment boundary before assigning authorship; one HTML paragraph can join genuine commentary to a disputed catena fragment, so a paragraph-level match and shared sermon heading do not prove uniform ownership. Record uncertainty at the claim level; a reported alternative attribution does not authorize silently reassigning the fragment. Record that ownership and stance before using a quotation or authoritative paraphrase. Check translator note-call boundaries even inside a single HTML paragraph: a note may flag a disputed fragment beginning midway through otherwise genuine material. Paragraph-level matching does not establish uniform authorship; retain the secure earlier witness and remove or explicitly qualify the disputed continuation. When the source's actual reading differs, disclose the difference or omit the witness; never quote a sympathetic fragment as false agreement with an argument the source rejects.
- Trace indirect testimony through the work actually opened: separate what the inspected author has read, what another author claims, and whether the inspected author agrees. Cite the opened work as a report unless the earlier text has also been verified; reopen that earlier text before presenting it as a direct witness, because reporting another author's appeal proves neither firsthand access nor assent.
- When a primary work is available only in a checked modern translation into another language, identify that language and edition and paraphrase transparently. Do not present a newly translated English sentence as a verified published quotation, and let the primary work control over a translator's inconsistent introductory summary.
- Track two separate gates: citation validity and teaching depth.

## Comparing grace, faith, and works across Christian traditions

Use this procedure when an Orthodox study needs a doctrinal comparison, especially with Augustine, Protestant confessions, or Roman Catholic teaching.

1. Begin with the biblical argument and the exact disputed proposition. Distinguish the source of salvation, reception of grace, justification, growth in holiness, perseverance, and judgment; changing the meaning of salvation midway creates a false disagreement.
2. Open each tradition's own authoritative account before describing it. For classical Reformed reasoning, inspect the *Heidelberg Catechism*, questions 60-64, at <https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism>. For Lutheran reasoning, inspect the *Augsburg Confession*, Articles IV, VI and XX, at <https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/>. Explain why these accounts exclude works from justification while insisting on the fruit of genuine faith; do not substitute popular antinomianism for their teaching or infer that Lutheran faith alone rejects sacraments.
3. Establish Orthodox teaching from received Coptic sources and close Scripture, not merely by negating another tradition. Explain grace-enabled cooperation as dependence on God's initiative and continuing action, never equal partnership or an earned supplement to Christ. In James 2, distinguish showing faith from faith being brought to completion through obedience; identify why an evidence-only explanation is insufficient as an exhaustive Orthodox reading without claiming its advocates reject love or holiness.
4. Read Augustine's actual work and containing argument, then separately establish its Coptic reception. *On Grace and Free Will*, <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1510.htm>, includes James and Sirach in chapter 3 and James's wisdom from above in chapter 46. Verify the particular passage and numbering before quoting. Do not turn agreement with his defense of grace into acceptance of every later proposal, or attribute later Augustinian schools to Augustine without evidence.
5. Compare Catholic definitions separately from Augustine. Read the *Catechism of the Catholic Church*, paragraphs 1987-2011, especially 1996-2002 on grace and free response and 2006-2011 on merit. The Vatican's grace section is <https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P6Z.HTM>. Acknowledge its teaching on participation, healing, cooperation and God's prior gift; do not portray Catholic justification as merely an external verdict or Catholic merit as purchasing initial grace. Verify any predestination claim against the actual Catholic definition rather than assigning a disputed Augustinian position to the whole Church.
6. Check a proposed narrative chronology against the complete biblical scene before building a theological contrast on it; a confession may explain an action already narrated rather than precede it. State a disagreement only where both the Coptic position and the other tradition's actual claim are supported. Distinguish a difference of vocabulary or emphasis from a doctrinal incompatibility. Do not invent a contrast to satisfy a request for differences; explain substantial agreement and qualify what remains unresolved. Keep disagreements connected to the passage rather than importing unrelated controversies.
7. Translate the distinction into lived faith with an example or analogy, then state its limit. Show how repentance, mercy and sacramental life belong to dependence on Christ without making salvation a performance tally. Preserve hope for weak, repentant faith and the difference between inability and refusal. Obtain review by a qualified Coptic theological reader for sensitive formulations.

## Direct quote gems

Consider direct patristic wording when it adds to a summary. When a guide, lesson, or audit uses a substantive Father, ask whether a verified sentence or tightly connected excerpt from that source would add authenticity, beauty, and weight. If yes, include it inline after the summary or paraphrase.

A direct quote is useful only when it does interpretive work. Do not quote as ornament. First explain what the Father is teaching in plain language, then let the Father's own words sharpen, confirm, or deepen that point.

Rules:
- Preserve the summary/paraphrase first; the direct quote sharpens and authenticates it, not replaces it.
- Prefer fuller, readable excerpts when the Father's own wording carries the theological point better than a clipped phrase. A good Bible-study quote is often one strong sentence or a tightly connected pair of clauses, not just a tiny gem. Still avoid long quote blocks that do not do real interpretive work.
- Follow the exact source/edition's live rights metadata and the current task's stricter limits. `quote_eligible` material may be quoted with citation. `paraphrase_only` means no direct reader-facing quotation by default; a numeric excerpt cap or the general preference for gems is not permission. A narrower source-specific exception requires explicitly documented permission for those exact bytes and must not contradict a current no-verbatim instruction. Otherwise paraphrase, or locate a separately authenticated quote-eligible edition without transferring its wording/rights to the restricted witness. `discovery_only`, `private_only`, and `blocked` material cannot become reader-facing quotes.
- Use the returned `source_deep_url` when available, falling back to `source_url` only when a deep link is unavailable or fails audit.
- Skip the quote rather than weaken provenance. A paraphrase with honest citation is better than an attractive but unsupported quote.

## Bible-study direct-quote citation style

For Bible-study guides, direct patristic quotes should carry a footnote marker, and the footnote itself should contain the citation with the direct public URL or `source_deep_url`. Do not make the reader click from the quote to a footnote and then again to a numbered source-list item. The footnote is the citation.

Preferred pattern:
- Use semantic footnote IDs in the Markdown, such as `[^athanasius-incarnation]` rather than bare `[^1]`. Rendered output will still number the footnotes, but semantic IDs are easier to maintain.
- Define the footnote near the end of the guide with the full short citation and direct/deep URL, e.g. `[^basil-hexaemeron]: St. Basil the Great, *Hexaemeron*, Homily I on Genesis 1:1, <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/32011.htm>.`
- `## Sources and Further Reading` may still use numbered lists for readability, but do not add HTML anchors solely for quote footnotes and do not make quote footnotes hop internally to the source list.
- Do not use application-specific wikilinks for citation footnotes in reader-facing guide bodies. Use portable Markdown footnotes with direct citation URLs.

## Body use: three tiers for `Gems from the Fathers`

1. **Tier 1 direct.** A Father comments on this passage or book. Summarize the interpretation in clean prose, then include verified direct wording when possible under a `Gems from the Fathers 💎` heading, e.g. `Saint John Chrysostom, commenting on this passage, teaches that ... He calls this "...".`
2. **Tier 2 thematic.** No direct comment surfaced for the passage, but a Father speaks to the passage's theme, such as repentance, exile, temple, perseverance, pride, mercy, judgment, prayer, or worship. Name the actual work and explain the connection positively, e.g. `Saint John Chrysostom's teaching on repentance helps us recognize the danger here: ...` Identify it as a thematic parallel when needed to avoid implying direct commentary. Keep the unsuccessful direct-commentary search in the internal evidence, not a reader-facing absence disclaimer. Add verified direct wording only if it genuinely illuminates the theme.
3. **Tier 3 none.** Omit the patristic subsection entirely. No empty heading, no placeholder, and no absence statement in the body.

## Scoped absence evidence

Record an unproductive patristic search in the existing source pack or audit evidence, scoped to the sources actually consulted. Do not require a reader-facing absence sentence or a second `## Source notes` heading merely to prove research occurred. Add a short limitation in reader-facing sources only when needed to prevent a likely false inference, using the project's approved final source section.

Do not claim the Fathers never addressed the passage. Keep tooling, query mechanics and local paths out of reader prose. Useful editorial evidence can say: `No direct patristic commentary on Ezra 4 surfaced in the sources checked for this study.`

## Depth rule

A hollow or forced citation fails even if the citation is real. Omit an unhelpful `Gems from the Fathers 💎` subsection and keep scoped absence in editorial evidence rather than dressing a generic point as patristic.

A verified paraphrase may be sufficient. Consider direct wording when it adds interpretive force, spiritual texture or necessary qualification beyond the explanation. Keep fewer strong witnesses rather than summarized name lists, but no quote/line count governs publication. Source availability alone is not a reason to add a quote; removing a redundant quotation may improve the guide. The shared Bible-study quality contract governs substantive acceptance.
