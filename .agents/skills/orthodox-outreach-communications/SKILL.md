# Orthodox Outreach Communications

Use this skill for congregation announcements, short talks, WhatsApp messages, volunteer invitations, ministry updates, and slide outlines for Orthodox outreach ministries: homeless outreach, visitation, mercy ministry, food distribution, service days, and similar work.

## Core posture

Outreach communication should sound like the Church serving Christ in the neighbor, not a nonprofit campaign.

Start with:

- Scripture
- prayer
- obedience to Christ
- human dignity
- mercy and repentance
- the Church acting as the Body of Christ

Then give practical details.

## Language rules

Use language that preserves dignity:

- "neighbors," "people in need," "brothers and sisters," or context-specific human language
- avoid clinical, bureaucratic, or savior-complex phrasing
- avoid making the people served into a project or problem
- avoid centering the ministry team's generosity instead of Christ's command and mercy

## Standard short congregation update

1. **Spiritual opening**
   - Matthew 25, the Good Samaritan, Acts, the Fathers on mercy, or a relevant feast/fast.
   - Name the service as obedience to Christ.

2. **Purpose**
   - What the ministry does and why.
   - Keep this warm and concrete.

3. **Current progress**
   - Training completed, schedule, partnerships, budget, preparation, recent fruit.
   - Include only details that build trust.

4. **How people can participate**
   - Pray
   - Give
   - Prepare supplies/food
   - Join the outing/service
   - Invite others appropriately

5. **Confident invitation**
   - Close clearly. Do not end with hedging.

## Reusable lines

- "Not everyone has to go in person to be part of the service. Some will pray, some will give, some will prepare, and some will go. All of that matters."
- "This is not public relations, and it is not simply social work. It is obedience to Christ in the person in front of us."
- "The direction is clear. The invitation is open. Come be part of it."

## Slide guidance

For congregation screens:

- use few words and large type
- one idea per slide
- avoid dense paragraphs
- use respectful Orthodox visual language: cross, icon-framing motifs, warm church colors, simple symbols of mercy
- do not use exploitative images of people in need
- if using photos, use permissioned, dignified, non-sensational images

## Checklist

Before finalizing:

- Did the message begin from Christ rather than logistics?
- Are people served described with dignity?
- Is the ask clear?
- Are dates/times/budget/training claims concrete and accurate?
- Can people participate even if they cannot attend in person?
- Does the close invite action with confidence?
