# Orthodox Depth Bar

## Shallow

- Generic spiritual application that could fit any passage or saint.
- Fathers mentioned only in bibliography or metadata.
- Scripture cross-links listed but not interpreted.
- Lectionary facts listed without spiritual meaning.
- A hollow or forced patristic citation, or a generic point dressed as patristic.
- Patristic material is entirely summarized even when a quote-eligible source offers a short phrase that would ground the Father's own voice.
- Language notes are used as decoration: isolated word definitions, Greek/Hebrew/Aramaic trivia, generic English-phrase explanations, or translation comments that do not change how the passage is read.
- Machine-status phrasing in reader-facing notes.

## Acceptable

- Explains the actual passage/topic accurately.
- Uses direct patristic commentary when it exists, or an honestly framed thematic patristic witness when it genuinely illuminates the passage.
- Keeps summary/paraphrase as the interpretive frame and includes a fuller direct quote when verified wording is available, source status allows it, and the Father's own sentence adds authenticity, weight, and spiritual texture.
- Gives practical application tied to the topic.
- Avoids false claims and names uncertainties in reader-facing language.
- Omits `### Gems from the Fathers 💎` when no genuine direct or thematic patristic material surfaced for that passage, then records scoped absence in Source notes.

## Strong

- Commentary speaks directly to the verses, feast, saint, icon, or doctrine just presented.
- Patristic material does interpretive work in the body, not merely source padding.
- Uses a few strong patristic anchors with the Father's own short verified words where possible, rather than many summarized names.
- Christological, ecclesial, sacramental, ascetical, and pastoral implications are explicit where relevant.
- Language and translation-shape notes, when present, reveal something the reader would otherwise miss and serve the passage's theology, worship, patristic reading, pastoral guardrail, or spiritual fruit. In NT work, strong guides actively notice original Greek or Aramaic terms that materially deepen interpretation, such as Logos, Paraklētos, or distinct Greek words flattened into one English rendering, without forcing unsupported distinctions.
- Spiritual application is concrete and actionable for adults, either woven into the explanation or substantial enough for its own `### For our life` subsection.
- Lectionary and Synaxarium material is verified or clearly marked with scoped uncertainty. A commemorated saint, feast, or liturgical day tied to the passage is named and allowed to shape the study when evidence exists.
- Teaching material is gathered in the final `## Spiritual Fruit 🍇` / `## Teaching Notes`, not scattered through the body. It should function as a coherent whole-study teaching guide, not a pile of relocated fragments.

## Excellent

- The whole artifact has a controlling spiritual burden that governs structure, sources, examples, and application.
- Every major passage or section presses the hearer toward repentance, worship, prayer, discernment, mercy, humility, watchfulness, or deeper union with Christ.
- Difficult texts are handled honestly and pastorally, with guardrails placed near the risky verses instead of hidden at the end.
- The lesson or guide feels Orthodox in structure, imagination, and spiritual instinct, not generic Christian with Orthodox decorations.
- The Fathers are present with both interpretation and small verified phrases that make the tradition feel embodied rather than merely reported.
- The prose has movement and beauty: the reader can follow a clear spiritual arc, technical terms are explained without condescension, and the writing invites prayer rather than merely displaying research.
- A modern adult keeps reading because the prose is concrete, warm, and text-rooted rather than padded, performative, or checklist-like.
- Spiritual Fruit is concrete enough to shape prayer, confession, teaching, or a real decision of the heart.
- Iconography, maps, manuscripts, or visual material deepen the reader's understanding when used; they are not ornamental fillers.

## Hollow citation rule

A valid citation can still fail the depth bar if it does not illuminate the specific passage or topic. Citation validity and teaching depth are separate gates. Tier 3 omission in the body does not lower the score; what fails is a hollow or forced citation, a generic point dressed as patristic, machine-status phrasing, or avoiding all direct patristic wording when a reliable short quote gem is available and would strengthen the teaching.

Teaching material belongs in the final `## Spiritual Fruit 🍇` and `## Teaching Notes` sections, not scattered through the body as lesson-plan fragments. Teaching Notes should function as a coherent whole-study teaching guide.
