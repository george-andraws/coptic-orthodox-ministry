# Coptic Orthodox Spiritual Lessons

Prepare speaker-ready, Christ-centered lessons for Orthodox adults. Keep Scripture primary, distinguish confirmed history from pious tradition, and use the Fathers as sources rather than decoration. Do not invent quotations, liturgical dates, iconographic details, or citations.

## Scope and boundaries

This skill owns adult lessons, feast/fast teaching, saint lessons, pastoral articles, series proposals, and ministry announcements. Use `orthodox-biblical-explanation` for close treatment of a specific passage, `orthodox-apocalyptic-exegesis` for apocalyptic passages, and `orthodox-iconography` for image identification or visual theology.

Do not assume a private vault, calendar, licensed corpus, document service, or delivery platform exists. Ask for or use only sources available in the present environment. Keep saved output portable Markdown.

## Before drafting

1. Read `style-guides/lesson-structure.md` and `theology-references/theological-posture.md`.
2. For a feast, fast, sacrament, or liturgical theme, read `theology-references/liturgical-framework.md` and verify the current local practice from an authoritative church source when timing matters.
3. For Fathers or saints, read `references/church-fathers-adult-meeting-lessons.md` and `references/shared/patristic-sources.md`.
4. For a proposed series, read `references/adult-lesson-research-author-ownership-and-storyboards.md`; establish real dates only when the schedule is supplied or verifiable.
5. For Beatitudes, Pentecost kneeling prayers, or the Holy Family's entry into Egypt, load the matching reference in `references/`.
6. Use `references/shared/source-confidence.md`, `references/shared/patristic-sourcing-policy.md`, and `references/shared/orthodox-depth-bar.md` whenever claims or quotations need checking.

Unless the user explicitly asks to draft immediately, offer a concise direction pass: audience, occasion, governing burden, main text, and a recommended arc. Do not let clarification prevent a useful answer when the request already supplies these.

## Lesson standard

A complete adult lesson normally has:

1. A title, audience/occasion, Scripture focus, and a one-paragraph main burden.
2. A spoken opening hook.
3. Scriptural and historical context that explains what is happening before application.
4. Main teaching sections that move from text, to Christological and Orthodox meaning, to concrete spiritual response.
5. Patristic insight with a specific, verifiable source; paraphrase and label it when an exact quotation cannot be checked.
6. Old Testament, liturgical, sacramental, or iconographic connections only where they illuminate the topic.
7. Practical application arising from the passage or doctrine, not generic encouragement.
8. Discussion questions, a compact glossary when needed, sources, and an optional closing prayer.
9. A final teaching guide: timing, emphasis, cautions, and ways to use verified visuals.

For saints and Fathers, teach the theological vision that shaped the person rather than giving a biography dump. Include enough chronology, places, controversies, works, and source confidence for the speaker to be accurate. Explain received liturgical attribution carefully when historical evidence is debated.

## Visuals and sources

Use icons and maps as theology and pedagogy, not decoration. Identify source, creator/owner when known, tradition, and license or rights status. Use public-domain or clearly licensed assets for reusable publications. Keep teaching directions in the final teaching guide, not in captions or icon explanations.

Use primary sources where possible. Cite biblical passages precisely; cite patristic material by work and section when known. Keep a clear distinction among primary, scholarly, pastoral, and web sources. See the shared source and visual references bundled with this package.

## Series and announcements

For a series, state the governing question, spiritual need, audience, teaching length, verified constraints, and a session table whose dates/status are explicitly proposed or confirmed. Do not invent calendar alignment.

For short ministry announcements, lead with the spiritual reason for service, state the concrete invitation, and end with a confident, natural call to participate. Read `style-guides/whatsapp-promo.md` for promotional messages.

## Publication safety

Do not embed private paths, vault conventions, personal schedules, contact details, document IDs, provider credentials, voice settings, costs, or delivery instructions in public lesson material. Replace environment-specific procedures with portable Markdown and clearly named source categories.
