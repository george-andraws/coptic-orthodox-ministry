# Orthodox Source Confidence

Use this reference to separate what is known, received, likely, uncertain, or merely useful as a discovery lead.

## Confidence labels

- **Confirmed:** verified directly from Scripture, primary patristic text, council/canon, liturgical text, Synaxarium entry, service book, or a reliable scholarly edition.
- **Received tradition:** held or prayed in the Church, even when modern historical reconstruction is more complex. State it reverently and distinguish it from documentary proof.
- **Likely:** supported by credible secondary sources but not yet verified in the primary source.
- **Uncertain:** plausible, but evidence is incomplete, contradictory, or outside the current check.
- **Discovery lead:** useful place to search, but not enough for final attribution.

## Rules

1. Do not turn a discovery lead into a final claim.
2. Do not quote a Father unless the wording is verified.
3. Do not attribute modern pastoral commentary to the ancient Fathers.
4. Do not assert exact lectionary placement, rite order, or Synaxarium date without checking a reliable source.
5. When uncertain, use humble wording: "appears," "likely," "the tradition commonly presents," or "this needs verification."
6. A beautiful claim that is not grounded should be omitted or marked as uncertain.
