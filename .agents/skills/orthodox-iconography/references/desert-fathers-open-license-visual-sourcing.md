# Desert Fathers open-license visual sourcing

Use this reference when selecting reusable images for lessons on St Antony, St Macarius, the Desert Fathers, Egyptian monasticism, or the sayings tradition.

## Recommended source pattern

Prefer 2–3 complementary assets rather than many decorative images:

1. a Coptic or Egyptian saint icon when file-level rights are clear;
2. a teaching/synaxis scene showing elder–disciple relationships;
3. optionally a manuscript, historical map, monastery, or paired-saint image that adds context.

For every candidate, record:

- exact individual image page URL;
- direct asset URL when supplied by the repository;
- creator, date, tradition, and physical or publication provenance;
- exact license/rights statement and any jurisdictional caveat;
- only visually verified, safely teachable iconographic details;
- uncertain identities, inscriptions, or traditional attributions that must not be taught as fact.

## Strict rights triage

Accept only when the individual asset page clearly says public domain or gives a specific Creative Commons license. A repository/category/site-level reputation is not enough.

Reject or defer when:

- a modern iconographer, parish, monastery, museum, auction, or commercial page has no explicit reuse grant for the individual image;
- an anonymous work is dated broadly into the twentieth century and public-domain status depends on an unknown death date;
- a Commons PD-Art page warns that the underlying public-domain rationale or United States tag is incomplete and the chronology does not independently settle it;
- the old artwork is public domain but the available photograph may contain creative choices and no reuse status is supplied;
- provenance is merely a generic category, image-search result, or unattributed repost.

A faithful mechanical reproduction of a clearly public-domain two-dimensional work may be usable when the repository explicitly marks the file PD-Art/Public Domain Mark. Preserve the repository's jurisdictional caveat rather than converting it into a worldwide guarantee.

## Reproduction and provenance distinctions

Distinguish these layers:

- **underlying object:** original icon/manuscript and its creator/date;
- **published reproduction:** plate, drawing, scan, or photograph and its creator/publication date;
- **current file:** crop or mechanical derivative hosted by the repository.

For a crop, verify the parent file's provenance and rights. A mechanical crop does not cure unclear rights in its source.

If a nineteenth-century catalog reproduces an older icon, identify the catalog artist/date as the image provenance while describing the underlying icon's creator/date as unknown unless independently established. Treat phrases such as “traditionally attributed to Rublev” as historical provenance, not secure authorship.

## Visual verification and teaching cautions

Inspect the actual image before explaining it. Teach only what is visible or settled by reliable file metadata.

Useful, safe observations may include:

- halo, monastic hood, staff, scroll, blessing or prayer gesture;
- elder seated with disciples kneeling or listening;
- desert dwelling, rocky landscape, raven and bread, or Christ above;
- contrast between liturgical vesture and extreme ascetic appearance.

Do not identify animals, demons, inscriptions, side scenes, or individual saints from low-resolution features alone. If metadata supplies an identity that the image itself cannot confirm, say so.

Hagiographic episodes such as the raven feeding Paul of Thebes should be labeled received hagiographic/iconographic tradition, not documentary reportage. A scene of an elder teaching disciples should not automatically be called a formal liturgical synaxis.

## Wikimedia rate-limit fallback

When direct `upload.wikimedia.org` inspection returns HTTP 429:

1. keep the exact direct asset URL returned by Commons API metadata;
2. open the individual Commons file page and inspect its rendered preview;
3. verify creator/date/source/license from the page or MediaWiki API;
4. inspect the parent file when the selected file is a crop;
5. do not treat a transient direct-media 429 as evidence that the asset or browser workflow is unusable.

The durable result is the verified file page plus metadata; do not download an asset when the user asked only for sourcing.

## Output shape

For each selected asset, give:

1. title/tradition/date;
2. exact image page and direct asset URL;
3. creator and provenance;
4. license/rights with any caveat;
5. safely teachable details and explicit cautions.

Finish with a short rejected-assets note naming the rights defect, especially for attractive Coptic sources that lack explicit reuse permission.
